### Category 1: Machine Learning & Classification Fundamentals

#### Q1: What type of machine learning problem does this project solve?
**A:** It is a supervised binary classification problem under extreme class imbalance. The model learns to map input transaction feature vectors $\vec{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{30}$ to a binary label $y \in \{0, 1\}$, where $0$ represents legitimate and $1$ represents fraudulent.

#### Q2: What are the input features in the Kaggle Credit Card Fraud dataset?
**A:** There are 30 features: `Time` (seconds elapsed), `Amount` (transaction amount), and `V1` through `V28`. Features `V1`–`V28` are numerical values generated by Principal Component Analysis (PCA) applied by the original dataset authors to preserve cardholder confidentiality.

#### Q3: Why don't features V1 through V28 have human-readable names like "merchant" or "location"?
**A:** To protect sensitive personal cardholder and banking data, the researchers applied Principal Component Analysis (PCA). PCA transforms the original high-dimensional feature space into orthogonal linear combinations that maximize variance while completely concealing the semantic identity of the underlying features.

#### Q4: Why is it critical not to fabricate real-world meanings for V1–V28 in the project report or UI?
**A:** Falsely claiming that an anonymized PCA component like $V_{14}$ represents "merchant category" or "shipping address" is technically dishonest and academically fraudulent. Respecting the true nature of PCA components maintains scientific integrity. Any human-friendly metadata (e.g. channel or city) must be strictly designated as an educational demo/simulation layer.

#### Q5: What is the difference between supervised learning and anomaly detection?
**A:** Supervised learning uses labeled training examples ($y \in \{0, 1\}$) to learn decision boundaries. Anomaly detection (like Isolation Forest or One-Class SVM) models the distribution of normal data only and flags outliers without requiring explicit fraud labels. Because our dataset has confirmed ground-truth fraud labels, supervised learning achieves superior discriminative precision.

### Category 2: Class Imbalance & Evaluation Metrics

#### Q6: Why is plain classification accuracy a useless metric for this dataset?
**A:** In this dataset, 284,315 transactions are legitimate and only 492 are fraudulent (~0.17%). A naive model that predicts "legitimate" for every transaction scores:
$$\text{Accuracy} = \frac{284,315}{284,807} \approx 99.83\%$$
It looks outstanding on paper but detects zero fraud, making it completely useless in a real financial system.

#### Q7: Define Precision and Recall in the context of fraud detection.
**A:**
- **Precision:** Of all transactions flagged as fraud by the model, how many were genuinely fraudulent?
  $$\text{Precision} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP}}$$
  Low precision causes customer friction and wasted investigator hours on false alarms.
- **Recall (Sensitivity):** Of all actual fraudulent transactions in the dataset, how many did the model successfully detect?
  $$\text{Recall} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}}$$
  Low recall means real criminals got away with stolen funds.

#### Q8: Why is Recall generally prioritized over Precision in fraud systems?
**A:** In financial risk management, the cost of a False Negative (missed fraud resulting in direct chargebacks, regulatory fines, and lost cardholder trust) is typically order of magnitudes higher than the operational cost of a False Positive (a temporary fraud alert or verification SMS).

#### Q9: What is the F1-Score, and why use the harmonic mean rather than arithmetic mean?
**A:** F1 is the harmonic mean of Precision and Recall:
$$F_1 = 2 \cdot \frac{\text{Precision} \cdot \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}}$$
The harmonic mean penalizes extreme imbalances between the two metrics heavily. If Recall is 99% but Precision is 1%, the arithmetic mean would misleadingly report 50%, whereas the harmonic mean correctly drops to ~2%.

#### Q10: What is ROC-AUC, and what does it measure?
**A:** Receiver Operating Characteristic Area Under Curve (ROC-AUC) measures the probability that the classifier ranks a randomly chosen positive (fraud) instance higher than a randomly chosen negative (legitimate) instance across all possible classification thresholds. A random guess has an AUC of 0.50; a perfect classifier scores 1.00.

#### Q11: Why is Precision-Recall AUC (PR-AUC / Average Precision) preferred over ROC-AUC on severely imbalanced datasets?
**A:** ROC curves plot True Positive Rate against False Positive Rate ($\text{FPR} = \frac{\text{FP}}{\text{FP} + \text{TN}}$). Because True Negatives ($\text{TN}$) are huge (>284,000), even thousands of false alarms leave $\text{FPR}$ close to zero, making ROC curves look deceptively optimistic. In contrast, the Precision-Recall curve plots Precision against Recall without including $\text{TN}$, giving a rigorous, honest view of minority-class detection performance.

#### Q12: What is the Brier Score?
**A:** The Brier score measures the mean squared difference between predicted probabilities and actual binary outcomes:
$$\text{Brier} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (p_i - y_i)^2$$
It assesses probability calibration. A Brier score near 0.0 indicates well-calibrated confidence probabilities.

### Category 3: Imbalance Handling & SMOTE

#### Q13: How does SMOTE work mathematically?
**A:** Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE) synthesizes new minority instances along line segments joining existing minority samples. For each minority sample $\vec{x}_i$, it finds its $k$-nearest neighbors among minority points, selects one neighbor $\vec{x}_{zi}$ randomly, and interpolates:
$$\vec{x}_{\text{synthetic}} = \vec{x}_i + \lambda (\vec{x}_{zi} - \vec{x}_i) \quad \text{where } \lambda \in [0, 1]$$

#### Q14: How does Cost-Sensitive Class Weighting work as an alternative to SMOTE?
**A:** It modifies the loss function during model training by assigning a higher loss multiplier to minority class errors:
$$L = - \left( w_1 \cdot y \log p + w_0 \cdot (1-y) \log(1-p) \right)$$
It requires no synthetic sample creation and no additional memory footprint.

#### Q15: In your experiments, which approach performed better: Class Weights or SMOTE?
**A:** Tuned Random Forest with SMOTE achieved higher F1 (0.8324 vs. 0.8214) and higher Recall (75.79% vs. 72.63%) than Random Forest with class weights alone, because synthetic interpolation enriched the sparse minority decision boundaries.

### Category 4: Data Leakage & Preprocessing

#### Q16: What is data leakage, and why is it dangerous?
**A:** Data leakage occurs when information from outside the training partition improperly influences model construction. It creates artificially inflated validation performance that drops catastrophically when deployed to unseen real-world production transactions.

#### Q17: Where could data leakage occur in this project, and how did you prevent it?
**A:** Data leakage could occur in two places:
1. **Scaler Leakage:** If `StandardScaler` is fitted on the whole dataset before splitting, the test set's mean and variance leak into training. We prevent this by performing stratified splitting first and fitting the scaler strictly on the training partition.
2. **SMOTE Leakage:** If SMOTE is applied before train/test splitting, synthetic points created from test-fold neighbors leak into the training fold. We prevent this by applying SMOTE strictly to the training fold, and inside cross-validation folds using `imblearn.pipeline.Pipeline`.

#### Q18: Why scale only Time and Amount, but not V1 through V28?
**A:** Features `V1`–`V28` are the direct output of PCA. Performing PCA inherently involves centering and standardizing the features, so `V1`–`V28` already have zero mean and unit variance. `Time` (0 to 172,000) and `Amount` (0 to 25,000) are unscaled raw measurements, so they require `StandardScaler` to prevent numeric domination.

#### Q19: Why drop duplicate rows in the dataset?
**A:** The raw dataset has 1,081 exact duplicate rows. Retaining duplicate rows could place identical copies of a rare fraud transaction into both train and test splits, creating an artificial memorization shortcut. Dropping duplicates ensures conservative, honest evaluation.

### Category 5: Tree Ensembles & Random Forest

#### Q20: What is a Random Forest, and why is it well-suited for fraud detection?
**A:** Random Forest is an ensemble of de-correlated decision trees trained via bootstrap aggregating (bagging) and random feature sub-sampling. It excels on tabular fraud data because it captures complex non-linear feature interactions, handles high-dimensional PCA components robustly, and resists overfitting compared to individual deep decision trees.

#### Q21: What hyperparameters did you tune in your Random Forest?
**A:** Using `RandomizedSearchCV` with 3-fold stratified cross-validation on an `imblearn` pipeline, we tuned:
- `n_estimators`: number of decision trees (100, 200, 300)
- `max_depth`: maximum tree depth (10, 16, None) to prevent excessive leaf specialization
- `min_samples_split`: minimum samples required to split an internal node (2, 5)
- `min_samples_leaf`: minimum samples at a leaf node (1, 2)

#### Q22: How does Random Forest calculate probability scores?
**A:** For a binary classification forest of $B$ trees, the fraud probability is the proportion of individual trees that vote for the fraud class:
$$P(\text{Fraud} \mid \vec{x}) = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{b=1}^B I\left(\hat{y}_b(\vec{x}) = 1\right)$$

### Category 6: Threshold Optimization & Risk Scoring

#### Q23: Why shouldn't a fraud detection system always use a 0.50 decision threshold?
**A:** The 0.50 threshold assumes equal misclassification costs ($C_{\text{FP}} = C_{\text{FN}}$). In banking, missing a €10,000 fraud ($C_{\text{FN}}$) is far more severe than sending a verification push notification for a false alarm ($C_{\text{FP}}$). Adjusting the threshold allows the institution to tune sensitivity based on its risk appetite.

#### Q24: What happens to Precision and Recall when you lower the threshold from 0.50 to 0.20?
**A:**
- Recall **increases** (catches more fraud because the barrier to flagging is lower).
- Precision **decreases** (more false alarms, higher analyst workload).
- In our test data, lowering the threshold to 0.20 increased Recall to 78.9% while False Positives rose from 6 to 15.

#### Q25: How does SENTINEL compute its Risk Score (0–100)?
**A:** The model output probability $P(\text{Fraud} \in [0, 1])$ is converted into a calibrated integer score:
$$\text{Risk Score} = \text{round}(P(\text{Fraud}) \times 100) \in [0, 100]$$
This is mapped to four operational risk tiers:
- `LOW` (0–30)
- `MEDIUM` (31–60)
- `HIGH` (61–80)
- `CRITICAL` (81–100)

### Category 7: Explainable AI & SHAP

#### Q26: What is SHAP and what theoretical foundation does it have?
**A:** SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) is a model-agnostic and tree-specific attribution technique grounded in cooperative game theory (Shapley values). It measures the marginal contribution of each feature to the difference between the model's prediction and the expected base value across all feature coalitions.

#### Q27: What is SHAP TreeExplainer?
**A:** `TreeExplainer` is an optimized $O(TLD^2)$ algorithm (where $T$ is trees, $L$ is leaves, $D$ is depth) that computes exact Shapley values for tree-based models (Random Forest, XGBoost) in seconds, rather than exponential time.

#### Q28: Which features were the most influential in your trained model according to SHAP?
**A:** Features $V_{14}$, $V_{12}$, $V_{10}$, and $V_{17}$ were the strongest fraud indicators. Strongly negative values in $V_{14}$ and $V_{12}$ pushed the model's fraud probability up by as much as +0.35 to +0.45.

#### Q29: How did you phrase the SHAP explanations to ensure technical honesty?
**A:** Explanations strictly reference the mathematical PCA components and their numeric values:
*"Component V14 (value: -4.28) increased the fraud risk score by +0.32."*
We deliberately avoid hallucinating real-world narratives like "merchant location triggered the alert".

### Category 8: Software Architecture & Database

#### Q30: Why introduce an SQLite database instead of keeping data only in memory?
**A:** In a production fraud operations center, transactions, alerts, and analyst notes must persist across application restarts. SQLite provides an ACID-compliant, lightweight relational datastore that tracks transaction metadata, alert statuses (`Open`, `Escalated`, `Resolved`), and analyst audit logs.

#### Q31: How does your database layer handle serverless environments like Vercel?
**A:** Vercel serverless execution environments have read-only file systems except for `/tmp`. In `src/config.py` and `src/database.py`, we detect serverless execution (`VERCEL` or `AWS_LAMBDA_FUNCTION_NAME`) and automatically route the SQLite database to `/tmp/sentinel.db`.

#### Q32: What is the purpose of the Simulation Engine?
**A:** Because real bank payment networks cannot be queried in a student project, the Simulation Engine generates synthetic high-velocity payment streams using empirical distributions and injects burst-attack fraud signatures, giving evaluators a realistic live monitoring experience.

#### Q33: What is "What-If" Analysis in SENTINEL?
**A:** What-If analysis evaluates a baseline transaction, allows an analyst to modify hypothetical attributes (e.g. escalating transaction amount or shifting time), and re-evaluates the model to display the score delta. An explicit disclaimer clarifies that this demonstrates mathematical model sensitivity, not real-world causality.

### Category 9: API & Vercel Deployment

#### Q34: What is the benefit of including a FastAPI REST backend?
**A:** Separating the machine learning pipeline from the UI allows external microservices, payment gateways, and banking apps to consume predictions via standardized HTTP endpoints (`POST /predict`, `POST /predict/batch`).

#### Q35: How did you make this project deployable on Vercel?
**A:** We created a `vercel.json` configuration defining `@vercel/python` and mounted `api/index.py` as an ASGI entrypoint. In `api/index.py`, FastAPI serves both the JSON REST API and an embedded responsive HTML/Tailwind web portal, enabling 100% serverless deployment on Vercel without requiring WebSockets.

#### Q36: Why can't Streamlit be deployed natively on Vercel Serverless?
**A:** Streamlit requires long-lived stateful processes, bi-directional WebSockets, and continuous Python server loops. Vercel Serverless executes transient request-response HTTP invocations that terminate after execution. Our architecture solves this by providing FastAPI for Vercel, and Streamlit for local and containerized deployments.

#### Q37: How did you optimize the model artifact size for GitHub and Vercel?
**A:** A standard 300-tree Random Forest model was 45.8 MB uncompressed. Using `joblib.dump(..., compress=3)`, we compressed the artifact to **13.4 MB** (a 71% reduction) with identical floating-point predictions and sub-second load times.

### Category 11: Real-World Limitations & Industry Practice

#### Q40: What are the main limitations of the 2013 Kaggle Credit Card dataset?
**A:**
1. **Temporal Decay:** The dataset is from 2013; modern payment ecosystems feature contactless NFC, UPI/instant payments, cryptomarkets, and 3D-Secure 2.0.
2. **Lack of Behavioral Context:** Anonymized PCA components lack cardholder history (e.g. average 30-day velocity, standard deviational distance from home, device fingerprint).

#### Q41: How are fraud models deployed and maintained in real banks?
**A:**
- **Inference Latency:** Models must score transactions within 50–100 milliseconds at the payment gateway.
- **Rules Engine Hybrid:** Real systems run a fast rules engine (e.g., hard decline for sanctioned countries) followed by an ML scoring model.
- **Model Monitoring & Concept Drift:** Distribution shift (e.g. population stability index) is monitored daily, and models are retrained weekly or monthly on newly settled chargeback data.

#### Q42: What is the difference between Batch Analysis and Real-Time Stream Scoring?
**A:** Real-time scoring evaluates transactions individually synchronously during authorization (under 100ms). Batch analysis processes settlement logs offline (e.g. nightly) to detect post-authorization fraud rings and coordinate recovery.

#### Q43: What is the risk of model feedback loops in fraud detection?
**A:** When a model declines suspicious transactions, those transactions never settle, meaning we never observe whether they would have been actual fraud or legitimate customer activity. This creates label bias in future training datasets that requires selective random approval exploration.

#### Q44: If an examiner asks: "Can you change the threshold live right now?", what do you demonstrate?
**A:** In the Streamlit app under **"📈 Model Performance" $\to$ "🎛️ Threshold Tradeoff Playground"** or in **"🔍 Analyze Transaction"**, adjust the threshold slider from 0.50 to 0.10. Show how Recall immediately increases, catching borderline fraud transactions while dynamically recalculating False Positives and False Negatives.

#### Q45: Summarize the primary technical contribution of this project in one sentence.
**A:** SENTINEL elevates basic tabular classification into a defense-grade fraud intelligence platform by coupling a leak-free SMOTE Random Forest pipeline (F1: 0.8324, PR-AUC: 0.8096) with SHAP explainability, calibrated risk scoring, SQLite review workflows, and a dual Vercel/Streamlit deployment architecture.
